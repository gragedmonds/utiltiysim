# Neighborhood street generation

Greg's 8 October direction is to prioritize UtilitySim v2, restore the finished
v1 isometric artwork and make street layouts feel like real towns. The renderer
restoration is already on main. This increment adds an opt-in physical street
pattern, `neighborhood-streets/1`, rather than changing roads only in the picture.

## Verified references

- [OpenStreetMap highway hierarchy](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:highway)
  describes a road's role within its network. Use that hierarchy to distinguish
  connecting roads, neighborhood streets and individual-property access.
- [OpenStreetMap residential roads](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:highway=residential)
  distinguishes local residential access from primarily through traffic and
  individual driveways. Names, width, lanes, sidewalks and access are distinct
  attributes; appearance alone should not define network behavior.
- [NACTO intersection principles](https://nacto.org/publication/urban-street-design-guide/intersections/intersection-design-principles/)
  emphasizes compact, legible junctions and evaluating them within the wider
  network. Use this as qualitative layout guidance, not a claim that generated
  roads meet an engineering standard.

These references were reviewed on 8 October 2026. No OSM extract, tiles or real
customer/address data have been imported. Generation remains seeded and offline;
these sources inform the distinction between through routes, neighborhood access
and junction layout. Generated towns are not reproductions of particular places.

## Behavior and use

The existing generator already owns arterial/collector/local classes, separate
pavement/right-of-way widths, era-based grids/loops/courts, naming, parcels,
network routing and road-based legacy travel. Keep that ownership. The renderer
must draw these records rather than adjust streets independently of service
networks and properties.

The retained 570-premise demo (`town-ab2b8b3609f83517`) provides a first visual
baseline: 61 road segments, with 26 primary and 35 residential; 17 degree-one
road nodes; 15 residential segments longer than 300 m (longest 627.13 m); and
two junctions with a smallest adjoining angle below 45°. Its isometric overview
shows long parallel streets. These measurements describe this saved snapshot,
not a representative benchmark or proof that any individual dead end is wrong.
The next comparison must include varied new seeds and town sizes.

In Studio's new-simulation wizard, choose **Environment → Advanced environment
settings → Town & geography → Street pattern → neighborhoods**. The default
`legacy` retains prior behavior and identities. Old configuration documents that
omit the field load as legacy. The existing generated-town API also accepts:

```json
{"preset":"village","seed":"TOWN-042","houses":480,
 "overrides":{"town":{"street_pattern":"neighborhoods"}}}
```

The selected setting is preserved in the generated snapshot; its `source`
also records `streetModel: neighborhood-streets/1`. Create a new world from that
snapshot using the saved-town picker. No saved world, inhabited property,
observation history or existing demo is regenerated by changing this setting.
Changing the new model in the future requires a generator/model version update;
the model label describes this implementation, not an independently selectable
historical generator executable.

The new option reuses the existing arterial and collector skeleton, parcel
generator, street naming, utility routing and crew router. Inside suitable
blocks it places a local loop behind two rows of lots, connects it to actual
main roads at two separated points, then subdivides its interior. Thin or
unconnectable blocks keep main-road frontage rather than gaining an isolated
local network. The map boundary is never used as a substitute access road.

Illustrative layout rules: loop setback is two configured lot depths plus local
right-of-way; access joins aim for at least 55 degrees, with 75 m separation
within a neighborhood and 30 m from previously added main-road access points;
new interior junctions stay 25 m clear of each other and meaningful corners.
Interior cuts target 190/220/250 m long blocks by construction era and also
limit width using lot depth. These are generation heuristics, **not engineering
standards or hard maximum lengths for every final street segment**. Curved loop
sections, cropping and rejected unsafe cuts can leave longer segments.

## Acceptance evidence

`tests/test_neighborhood_streets.py` checks legacy identity, repeatability,
configuration validation, connected and planar roads, separated local
junctions, exact residential counts, parcel overlap, building containment,
unique addresses, source-connected service networks, depot-to-every-property
travel, and saved-world restart/replay across four utility observations.
Fixtures cover 20, 120, 480 and 2,000 houses, including 400 m arterial warp.

An additional 24-layout diagnostic sweep used six seeds at those four sizes,
with 0/130/400 m arterial warp: all stayed connected, had no un-noded crossings,
and placed the requested residential count. This is sampling, not proof that
every supported configuration has sufficient frontage.

The 480-house `TOWN-042` comparison changed the cropped road count from 61 to
149 and the longest local segment from 627.13 m to 413.70 m. The new town has
608 total premises, including nonresidential sites; counts outside the fixed
residential target legitimately change with frontage and land use. All sampled
new residential buildings were contained in their lots (the separate 2,000-home
fixture exceeded 99.8%). The regression test compares median local segment
lengths for this paired fixture without imposing that result on every seed.

Browser drivers:

```text
python scripts/check_neighborhood_settings.py --engine-python <engine-python> --out <new-output-directory>
python scripts/check_world_isometric.py --db <saved-world.sqlite> --viewer-dir packages/town-viewer/dist --out <new-output-directory>
```

Use a Python environment with Playwright/Chromium for the drivers. Settings
acceptance follows the actual Studio form, saves/reopens its draft and sends
the selected value to the generation API; it does not claim to run a full
utility year through the wizard. Map checks passed on 204-, 608- and
2,268-premise snapshots at 1440 and 1024 pixels, including four isometric
directions, plan view, 3D, selection, refresh and no idle 3D drawing. Original
world files and physical records stayed unchanged; no external requests or
browser errors occurred. Local evidence is in `out/streets/`.

## Remaining scope

This increment introduces connected neighborhood loops and blocks. More varied
deliberate cul-de-sacs, terrain/water barriers, regional street archetypes,
sidewalk/turn/access rules and real-map import remain future work. The old
generator's court templates remain available through `legacy`. Road-based
travel is verified in the existing router; consuming workforce reservations
and applying weather disruption in the durable v2 field lifecycle are separate
unfinished integration work. No enterprise queue is completed by a map change.
