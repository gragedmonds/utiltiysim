# Fairhaven: interactive 500-home town

This continuation expands the whole-block experiment into a separate, engine-backed town. It is an authored scaling demonstration, not a finished procedural city generator or approval of parity with the original Civic Atlas concept.

## Run the actual application

From the repository root, after the setup in [Run and verify](RUN_AND_VERIFY.md):

```sh
.venv/bin/python prototypes/civic-atlas/server.py --town500 --port 8041 --store out/civic-atlas-town500-v2
```

Open `http://127.0.0.1:8041/#welcome` or `http://127.0.0.1:8041/#map`. Use **Explore a place** to visit neighborhoods, Main Street, the school, church, workshops, parks and fields. Click individual roofs or search an address to inspect its saved property and daily utility observations. Water, Electricity and Gas display saved topology; they are not hydraulic/voltage engineering analyses. Pan and zoom keep the fixed orthographic camera. The artwork button switches to native geometry at the same position.

Brookfield remains a separate profile/store on 8040, including the [three residential samples](RESIDENTIAL_SAMPLES_2026_10_10.md). Do not reuse one profile's existing store for the other. The `--town500` server rejects an existing store from another profile. Loopback URLs refer to the machine running this server; a cloud browser requires that environment's preview/forwarding support.

## What exists

- 500 residential premises, 475 occupied homes and **1,185 residents in occupied households**. The raw occupant assignments including vacant homes total 1,251; that is not the resident count displayed in the shell.
- 25 nonresidential premises: 20 Main Street shops, an elementary school, a church, a service depot, light workshops and a pump site. All 525 properties retain unique source IDs.
- 1,500 physical utility assets and 10,500 observations from seven completed days on a fresh store. Jan 1–7 are completed; Jan 8 is the next physical day.
- A connected road graph with four boundary exits, separated eastern service access, four saved parks, 50 shared residential greens and seven surrounding fields.
- Twelve manually generated, registered block images across 58 placements: four residential templates cover 50 ten-home blocks; eight distinct commercial/civic/industrial plates cover the remaining illustrated blocks.

Solar and pools are source attributes, not random paint. Three solar slots repeat in each of 11 solar blocks (33 homes); two pool slots repeat in each of 11 courtyard blocks (22 homes). Their geometry/settings enter artwork registration. The setting changes are authored in the fixture before networks and daily profiles are initialized; the UI does not yet provide an implemented solar/pool editing workflow.

Fields and common greens are saved land-use descriptions. Crops, hedges, benches, parking markings, service vehicles and garden details do not create farm production, traffic agents, customers or utility demand. The church uses an explicit institutional property represented by the existing engine's supported service model, not a new church-specific occupancy scheduler.

## How the artwork works

`town500_world.py` defines roads, source parcels, footprint/frontage/appearance contracts, civic sites and land-use areas. `render-town-guide.mjs` captures exact-camera guides and companion metadata. The image tool supplies manually reviewed illustration; no generation request occurs while using the website.

`web/map-town-plates.js` normalizes each placement relative to its block center. Reuse permits translation only: source footprint, parcel, frontage, storeys, roof, solar, pools, terrain and declared facilities/parks/commons must match. It loads one texture per template and uses shared instances. A changed block falls back to native geometry; missing or wrongly framed template art affects that template's placements. Native source geometry continues to own picking and utility state. This is twelve shared illustrations, **not 500 uniquely generated images**.

Regenerate guides with the Fairhaven server running:

```sh
ATLAS_TEMPLATE=all node prototypes/civic-atlas/render-town-guide.mjs
```

The output directory is `out/civic-atlas-town500/guides/`. Each image and its matching metadata are published as `web/assets/civic-template-<templateId>.png` and `.json`. Do not update metadata merely to force mismatching artwork to load. Physical edits require reviewed replacement art or native fallback.

## Limits and next iteration

The complete town exposes repetition: a regular ten-by-six block grid, equal residential lot sizes, four repeated house arrangements and an abrupt rectangular rural transition. Detailed block art improves neighborhood views, but does not solve organic town planning. The next geographic pass should vary block dimensions and frontage, reserve irregular open land, introduce gradual rural edges and design farm tracks/access without inventing agricultural simulation.

The eastern sites suit a light-workshop/depot proof; the nearest residential lots are about 27 m away along their north/south edges, despite a wider western buffer. This is not a validated heavy-industrial siting model. School and park distance checks are straight-line diagnostics, not school-capacity or pedestrian-route guarantees.

Illustrations can still drift in roof height, doors, paving or planting despite registered source anchors. Sharp edge/detail review, usable driveways, decorative/native seam consistency, maximum-zoom resolution and occlusion remain important. Source-signature tests prove metadata compatibility, not pixel-perfect image compliance. The original concept remains the visual target.

Road sketching remains a browser draft/export, not an engine construction editor. Account/sign-in is an honest unconfigured menu, and external integrations remain unconfigured. Performance evidence is from Chromium software rendering in this environment; larger settlements and normal GPU hardware need separate measurement.

See [independent Fairhaven review](VISUAL_REVIEW_FAIRHAVEN.md) and the retained `check-town500.mjs`, `check-town-template-renderer.mjs`, and `test_town500_world.py` for review evidence.
